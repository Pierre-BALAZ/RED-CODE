Resuscitative hysterotomy

Perimortem caesarean section

Objective = relieve aortocaval compression to aid maternal resuscitation (delivery is the resuscitation, not an obstetric procedure)

Indications

Maternal cardiac arrest

ERC Guidelines 2025 — cardiac arrest in pregnancy. Seek the pregnancy-specific 4Ps (pre-eclampsia / eclampsia, puerperal sepsis, placental & uterine complications, peripartum cardiomyopathy). Resuscitative hysterotomy is time-sensitive: prepare early and perform as soon as possible at the site of arrest by a skilled team; the primary aim is maternal resuscitation. Read the full recommendations

Aim to deliver within 5 min of arrest.

≈ 4.5%Maternal survival to discharge (3/66) — the aim is maternal ROSC

Steps

Continue maternal CPR / ALS
Do not stop compressions. Assign one person solely to maternal resuscitation throughout.
Vertical midline abdominal incision
From just below the xiphoid to the pubic symphysis — through skin, fascia and peritoneum. Beware the bladder near the symphysis.
Open the uterus
Small incision in the lower uterine segment, then extend vertically to the fundus. Keep your non-dominant hand between the fetus and the uterine wall to avoid fetal injury.
Deliver the infant
Clamp the cord twice, cut between the clamps, and hand the infant to the neonatal team.
Baby delivered — and now?
Deliver the placenta & pack
Deliver the placenta, pack the abdomen, and continue maternal resuscitation.

And now?

Two patients now. Hand the newborn to the neonatal team and start newborn life support; continue maternal ALS and post-resuscitation care (multidisciplinary, urgent transfer).

FONA

Surgical cricothyroidotomy — front of neck access

Objective = restore oxygenation in a “can't intubate, can't oxygenate” (CICO) emergency

Indications

Can't intubate, can't oxygenate (CICO)

DAS 2025 guidelines — unanticipated difficult intubation. When Plan A (intubation), Plan B (supraglottic airway) and Plan C (face-mask) fail, declare CICO and move to Plan D: scalpel cricothyroidotomy (scalpel–bougie–tube). Surgical FONA is the recommended technique; cannula cricothyroidotomy has a high failure rate. Read the full recommendations

≈ 88–100%First-pass success — surgical (scalpel) technique
~ 63% failCannula cricothyroidotomy (NAP4) — not recommended

Steps

Declare CICO
Call for help, ensure full paralysis, 100% O₂. No more intubation attempts.
Identify the cricothyroid membrane
Extend the neck; locate the membrane with the laryngeal handshake. Impalpable CTM (obese / distorted): make an 8–10 cm vertical midline incision, then finger-dissect down to the membrane.
Stab incision through the membrane
Transverse stab through the cricothyroid membrane, then turn the blade 90° with the sharp edge pointing caudally.
Bougie
Slide the coudé-tip bougie through the incision into the trachea, angled caudally.
Railroad the tube
Advance a lubricated cuffed 6.0 tube over the bougie into the trachea, then remove the bougie.
Confirm & secure
Inflate the cuff, ventilate, and confirm placement with waveform capnography. Secure the tube.
